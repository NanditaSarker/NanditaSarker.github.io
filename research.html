Research

My research examines environmental exposures, their sources, and the health risks they pose.

Doctoral research

My doctoral research at the Texas A&M School of Public Health focuses on exposure to polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) in the United States. It combines national biomonitoring data from NHANES with a community-based environmental assessment in Texas to better understand where exposure comes from and what it may mean for health.

Community science and environmental health training

Since August 2026, I have been a Graduate Research Assistant with Together for a Better Tomorrow (TBT) at the Texas A&M School of Public Health, on a project funded by the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. I help develop and implement a Community Science and Environmental Health Training Program with community partners, conduct literature reviews and evidence synthesis to support program development, evaluation, and community engagement, and support activities that build community capacity to collect and interpret soil and water data.

Environmental health and safety

From June to December 2024, I was an Environmental Health and Safety Intern on the Laboratory Building Operations Team at Texas A&M University. I conducted and shadowed laboratory safety inspections and hazardous waste management procedures, restructured and expanded the EHS Laboratory Safety Manual using a risk assessment and management framework, and developed and updated laboratory safety and cryogenics training, high-hazard chemical SOPs, and new PI safety materials.
